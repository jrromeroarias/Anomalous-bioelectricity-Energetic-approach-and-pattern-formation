# Anomalous-bioelectricity-Energetic-approach-and-pattern-formation
This repository contains the computational framework, simulations, and analysis tools developed to investigate the coupled evolution of bioelectric and biomechanical processes during solid tumor growth.

# The proposed model integrates:
- Phase-field theory for tumor growth dynamics,
- Spherical harmonics for morphological asymmetry and anisotropy,
- Turing mechanisms for spatiotemporal pattern formation,
- Energetic formulations of electro-mechanical interactions.

Using this framework, we study how tumor growth drives the emergence of heterogeneous electrical structures characterized by spatially varying:

- Electrical biopotentials,
- Volumetric charge density,
- Surface charge density at the tumor–healthy tissue interface,
- Mechanical stress and anisotropy.

Our results suggest that bioelectricity and biomechanics are tightly coupled regulators of tumor self-organization. The simulations reveal the spontaneous formation of positive and negative charge-density regions that contribute to global tumor electronegativity and may influence invasion, metastasis, immune evasion, and resistance to anticancer therapies.

# Main Features
- Energetic modeling of tumor growth and bioelectricity.
- Spherical harmonic representation of tumor morphology.
- Phase-field simulations of heterogeneous tumor evolution.
- Analysis of electrical charge distributions and biopotentials.
- Quantification of anisotropy and intratumoral heterogeneity.
- Visualization tools for spatiotemporal bioelectric patterns.
- Reproducible computational workflows for cancer systems biology.

# Scientific Motivation
Abnormal bioelectricity is increasingly recognized as a fundamental hallmark of cancer. However, its relationship with tumor morphology, mechanical forces, and pattern formation remains poorly understood. This project provides a quantitative framework to investigate how electrical and mechanical processes coevolve during tumor growth and how their interaction may contribute to cancer progression and metastasis.
Applications:

- Mathematical oncology
- Cancer systems biology
- Biophysical modeling
- Bioelectric signaling
- Tumor biomechanics
- Pattern formation and self-organization
- Personalized cancer therapeutics
